# Simulation model, version 0.1

All physiological and decompression values are unvalidated gameplay outputs. The model must not be used as a real dive computer, training aid or medical prediction.

## Units and integration

The independent header `DiveModel.h` uses metres, seconds, newtons, kilograms, litres and bar. Unreal converts distances by 100 cm/m. The game applies physics in 20 ms steps. Pressure uses freshwater density 1000 kg/m³ and gravitational acceleration 9.80665 m/s². The scenario surface pressure is configurable, initially 0.965 bar; it is not a live weather measurement.

`P = P_surface + rho * g * depth / 100000`.

Inertial mass is the sum of the 80 kg body, every equipment item, ballast, cylinder gas and BCD gas; the default prepared loadout weighs 117 kg. See [LOADOUT.md](LOADOUT.md). Thrust, quadratic drag relative to the current, suit compression, remaining gas mass, ballast and BCD gas contribute to movement. Suit, body and lung displacements are deliberately approximate. There is no full hydrodynamic body/fin model yet.

Blocked full-body turns can now accept a collision-tested sideways head turn within an authored ±65 degree heading limit. Head and body orientation are stored separately; physical mouth position remains consistent with rendered gaze. Hands and fins retain body heading during this fallback. This is a game animation constraint, not a biomechanical model. See [HEAD_TURN.md](HEAD_TURN.md).

## Gas and buoyancy

Surface respiratory minute volume starts at 16 L/min and rises deterministically with effort, stress and fatigue. Ambient pressure scales tank consumption while breathing through the regulator. The optional `Exposure` input identifies body immersion, mouth submersion and regulator placement. The three breathing sources are regulator, ambient air and no supply. Removing the regulator above water permits air breathing without tank draw; removing it underwater or keeping an empty regulator in the mouth produces no supply. Inserting/removing it uses the remappable T action. Shore starts begin on ambient air; offshore starts use the regulator. BCD inflation draws gas from the same tank. BCD gas is stored as equivalent litres at one bar; its current volume is this quantity divided by ambient pressure. The selected BCD vents above its 12, 18 or 24 L capacity. Preparation fills up to 8 local litres from the cylinder (virtual supply in Exploration), without automatically compensating ballast. Empty cylinders cannot supply additional BCD gas.

Breath phase drives lung displacement, exhalation sound and bubble emission. Ascending bubbles use the cube root of the pressure ratio to change radius. The player must distinguish swimming upward (Space) from inflating the BCD (Q).

## Physiology

Heart rate and breathing rate approach targets derived from exertion, stress and fatigue. Stress responds to low gas, plan exceedance and rapid ascent. Cold exposure depends on immersion, depth temperature, air temperature, wind and suit choice. Simulated SpO2 responds to a finite normalized breath reserve; loss of supply no longer immediately lowers saturation. Below 60 ends non-Exploration play. This also applies to an empty regulator kept in the mouth on land. Exploration retains its SpO2 protection. The displayed ppO2 describes the available source mixture (zero for no supply), not alveolar oxygen. These are qualitative couplings, not clinical relationships.

The reserve component retained in `coupled_impairments_v2` stores reserve R in [0,1], initially 1. Without supply it spends `dt*(1+2*exertion+0.5*stress+0.5*fatigue)/120`. With supplied air or regulator gas it approaches 1 exponentially at 0.12/s, so briefly reinserting the regulator cannot reset it. Saturation target is `45+53*clamp(R/0.25,0,1)`. Without supply the target cannot exceed the current saturation; response rate is 0.035/s. With supply the response rate is 0.08/s. Exploration holds reserve and saturation at their initial values while retaining the immediate supply-loss warning. The reserve is displayed as a game percentage, never as seconds of safe breath-holding or measured oxygen litres.

This separation of supply, stored reserve and saturation is conceptually motivated by [Andersson and Evaggelidis (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298614/), who measured saturation and pulmonary-store changes during dynamic apnea in ten breath-hold divers. It does **not** reproduce or fit that study's measurements. Its constants are explicit gameplay choices, not individual tolerance estimates. It omits CO2 accumulation, chemoreflexes, actual pulmonary/blood/myoglobin volumes, pressure-dependent apnea gas exchange, delayed peripheral oximetry and post-apnea saturation nadirs. These remain necessary for a substantially more physiological model.

The thermocline is a smooth logistic transition, initially centred on 10 m with a 2 m transition scale, between 18 °C and 6 °C defaults. Surface/deep temperature, centre depth and transition scale are configurable in the environment menu. These values are scenario parameters, not Walensee measurements.

Air temperature is configurable from −10 to 40 °C (default 20). With immersed fraction I, the recorded environment temperature is `I*Twater+(1-I)*Tair`. Cooling uses the existing suit coefficient multiplied by `I*max(0,22-Twater)+(1-I)*0.05*(1+wind/12)*max(0,22-Tair)`. The air factor, wind effect and recovery toward 37 °C are qualitative; heat stress, suit drying and measured heat-transfer coefficients are absent. An upright 1.8 m body proxy estimates immersion, and a point 12 cm below the physical eye position determines whether the mouth is underwater. This is not a full anatomical or head-pose model. Waves can intermittently cover the mouth even when the eye is above water; those steps become no-supply states with the regulator removed. The finite reserve buffers short wave contacts; sustained or repeated insufficient ventilation can still deplete it.

## Nitrogen approximation

Sixteen nitrogen compartments use exponential uptake/release under constant pressure within each integration step. Public Bühlmann nitrogen half-times and coefficients are used with a fixed gradient factor of 0.8. The computed ceiling is the maximum compartment ambient pressure requirement converted to freshwater depth. No-supply intervals retain the existing tissue approximation; alveolar gas depletion and breath-hold kinetics are not independently modeled. Ambient-air intervals use surface pressure and 79% nitrogen. Displayed NDL is time at the current depth until the surface limit is reached. The separate ascent forecast described below projects stops; it does not change the NDL definition. Gas switching, repetitive dives and GF-low/high interpolation remain absent.

Reference for the published coefficient family and conventions: [Subsurface's decompression model](https://github.com/subsurface/subsurface/blob/master/core/deco.cpp). No Subsurface implementation code is included. Coefficients are numerical model data; integration and game logic here are independently implemented. The first compartment uses the 4-minute variant (a=1.2599, b=0.5050).

A separate gameplay safety-stop timer counts 180 seconds at 3–6 m after exceeding 10 m. It is not a substitute for the compartment ceiling. The mandatory modeled stop takes display priority over this optional timer.

### Ascent forecast

`Simulation/Decompression.h` projects a copy of the current nitrogen pressures to the surface at 9 m/min, visiting 3 m depth intervals. It uses the same fixed GF=0.80, freshwater pressure conversion and selected oxygen fraction as the live model. It analytically integrates the linear pressure ramp and solves the preceding constant-depth wait for every compartment. The longest required wait is rounded upward to a whole second. The first interval starts at the actual depth; if it cannot be traversed immediately, the first hold can be at that non-grid depth. Subsequent stops are at 3 m intervals. Holds and ascent travel contribute to total time to surface; the optional safety stop does not.

For a compartment with rate `k`, current pressure `P`, inspired pressure `I`, ascent-inspired-pressure rate `r`, travel time `t` and preceding hold `w`, the projected end pressure is `I + r*t - r/k*(1-exp(-k*t)) + (P-I)*exp(-k*(w+t))`. The fixed-GF allowed pressure at the destination is `ambient*(1+GF*(1/b-1))+GF*a`. The implementation solves that inequality rather than stepping a complete future dive at render frequency. Under these fixed-gas, fixed-GF ascent assumptions the margin has no interior minimum below its endpoints; the independent numerical tests also check the entire ascent in quarter-second increments.

The forecast refreshes at most once per simulated second and freezes with the simulation. It never changes the live compartments. A current ceiling violation, invalid inputs, depth beyond the 300 m implementation domain, or a projection exceeding six hours produces an explicit unavailable result, not a zero-duration clearance. Exploration has no forecast. The fourth wrist page shows next stop, estimated hold, estimated ascent time and current ceiling; the HUD shows a required stop above other dive instruments. Waiting discharges the live tissues, so estimates change with actual behavior rather than running as an independent countdown.

This is a **game forecast, not a validated real-world decompression planner**. It assumes continued breathing of the configured gas, does not establish that the cylinder contains enough gas, and does not include gas switches, repetitive-dive history, a GF-low/high curve, thermal/workload effects on tissue kinetics or individual susceptibility. Empty-cylinder and other warnings remain separate. Forecast values are live derived data; existing logs still record ceiling and NDL but do not persist the projected stop schedule. The UI currently offers air; the 32% oxygen numerical test cases exercise the solver and do not imply a selectable Nitrox loadout.

`Tests/DecompressionTests.cpp` independently re-integrates 20 profiles with fourth-order Runge–Kutta at no more than 0.25 s per step, using two surface pressures, two nitrogen fractions and five depth/duration combinations. It verifies every projected hold and ascent against the compartment limits, minimal whole-second waits, total elapsed forecast time, input isolation and invalid/unavailable cases. Additional checks cover the 50 bar gas-reserve estimate, ppO2, stress-dependent gas use, low-gas warnings and the transition through predicted NDL. The existing core tests retain gas/BCD balance, tissue uptake/release, safety-stop timing and ascent warnings. These are mathematical and software checks; independent physiological/clinical validation remains outstanding.

## Modes and persistence

Exploration uses unlimited gas, optional assisted trim, permanent HUD and no physiological impairment or tissue loading. Simulation uses finite gas, manual BCD, coupled impairment and optional HUD. Hardcore adds a cold/flow-dependent fictional regulator fault and suppresses automatic coaching. All three share the same core. Exact rules, parameters, limitations and validation are described in [MODES.md](MODES.md).

Samples are recorded once per second. Versioned JSON includes position, depth, gas, vital signs, warnings and temperatures. Every 30 samples, a temporary file is written and renamed to the session log. Completion, interruption and gas-exhaustion states are distinct. Repeated dives currently reset tissues; persistent repetitive-dive physiology is not implemented.

The logbook reads schemas 1–6. Schema 6 adds a loadout manifest and required mass/volume samples with physical consistency checks; see LOADOUT.md. Schema 5 requires coupled impairment and regulator-fault data and identifies `coupled_impairments_v2`; see MODES.md. Schema 4 added required `breath_reserve` in [0,1] and used `breath_reserve_v1`. Older files remain readable without displaying an invented reserve. Schema-3 samples record breathing source (0 regulator / 1 air / 2 none), regulator placement, mouth submersion, body immersion and mixed environment temperature. The chart cursor shows this context; old files do not invent missing context. The new no-supply warning uses bit 256, accepted from schema 3 onward. Schema 2 adds the actual dive start in UTC and an automated-test flag; legacy dates are extracted from the local-time filename without claiming UTC. Background jobs scan metadata and read the selected profile without accessing Unreal objects. The index retains summaries only. Unsupported or corrupt files are counted and skipped; `.tmp` files are ignored. Numeric types, finite values, ranges and nondecreasing sample times/distances are checked before plotting. Per-file limits are 64 MB and 100,000 samples; the reader does not truncate valid profiles silently. Automated runs are hidden from the player list.

The browser paginates newest filenames first and filters by mode. Four chart groups show the original physiology, gas, depth, temperature and distance metrics. Schema-5 capacity, narcosis, panic, decompression strain and fault loss are shown numerically at the cursor; the other new diagnostics remain in JSON. A shared mouse cursor selects the nearest recorded sample; warnings are the stored flags at that instant, and yellow markers indicate newly raised flags. Event arrows navigate to those transitions. Per-pixel extrema preserve narrow peaks when long profiles are drawn. No interpolation is used to invent missing vital signs. Empty records display an explicit empty state. The data remains read-only and does not resume physiology or alter a paused dive.

`Tools/TestLogbook.ps1` uses Unreal's [command-line automation runner](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/run-automation-tests-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US). Tests cover legacy/current formats, a 7,201-sample two-hour fixture, nearest-time lookup, missing and invalid values, ordering, unsupported versions, file indexing and isolated test-log filtering. This data-length test is not a two-hour live-game stability test. The `logbook` gameplay smoke scenario saves a real simulated dive, parses the output back, opens that file in the browser and captures all four chart groups in German/English.

## Water optics

The water surface uses Unreal's [Single Layer Water shading model](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine), with base scattering coefficients RGB `(0.00015, 0.00045, 0.00032)` and absorption `(0.0016, 0.00035, 0.00055)` per centimetre, and phase G `0.35`. Both coefficient vectors scale by `14 / configured visibility in metres`. These are illustrative optical settings, not measured Walensee water properties. Underwater visibility additionally uses a distance-attenuation post-process and height fog.

`Simulation/VisualMedium.h` supplies a shared smoothstep weight over 0–0.5 m below the moving surface for fog and camera tint/saturation. Both use the current frame's eye position, including breathing motion; body immersion and pressure depth remain separate. This half-metre transition is an artistic compromise to soften crossings, not physical refraction or a measured optical interface.

In air, volumetric particles have white albedo, additive fog colour is black and Sky Atmosphere supplies the aerial fog colour. Underwater, the sky ambient contribution fades to zero while the established green particle/albedo settings take over. This follows Unreal's [Sky Atmosphere and height-fog integration](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sky-atmosphere-component-in-unreal-engine) and [volumetric fog controls](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/volumetric-fog-in-unreal-engine). Air density is 0.002, 0.012 in rain, or 0.020 in storm; submerged density is `0.35 * 14 / visibility`, independent of the aerial weather density. Height falloff interpolates from 0.2 in air to 0.01 underwater. These are engine rendering parameters, not SI extinction measurements. The model handles non-finite optical inputs and clamps visibility to 2–40 m. Day/night lighting, baked shadows in aerial imagery and real-world optical acceptance remain separate unfinished work.

## Light sources and exposure

`Simulation/EnvironmentLight.h` couples the direct sun, underwater ambient source and camera exposure. The solar path remains an illustrative 12-hour day: sunrise 06:00, sunset 18:00 and maximum elevation 65 degrees. Hours wrap over 24 hours. This is **not** a Walensee astronomical ephemeris or a civil-time/date calculation. The sun remains below the horizon at night; Sky Atmosphere computes atmospheric extinction and Earth shadow. The source outside the atmosphere stays illuminated, allowing the sky to scatter light through sunset instead of switching it off at the horizon. Moon, stars and shoreline lighting remain absent.

The directional light uses 80,000 lux of white illumination above the atmosphere, multiplied only by the weather preset. Its source intensity no longer has a second sine-of-elevation multiplier: Unreal already attenuates and colors atmospheric sunlight ([Epic directional-light documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/directional-lights-in-unreal-engine); installed UE 5.8 `PrepareSunLightProxy` and `FAtmosphereSetup::GetTransmittanceAtGroundLevel`). The hand torch explicitly uses 1,000 lumens, inverse-square falloff, inner/outer half-angles of 12/27 degrees and a 24 m engine attenuation limit. The previous 14,000 unitless value was a legacy engine scale, not lumens. Unreal distinguishes [directional lux from local-light units](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/light-properties-in-unreal-engine). These values define the game equipment/environment; they are not a measured lamp model or lake irradiance record. The captured skylight uses neutral white and an artistic multiplier of 3; its captured source already responds to the sun and clouds, so daylight is not multiplied into its intensity a second time.

Solar-source weather factors are 1/0.55/0.3/0.12 and diffuse factors 1/0.65/0.4/0.25 for clear/overcast/rain/storm. The normalized ambient water source is `A = D * diffuseWeather * exp(-max(0, eyeDepth)*0.06*14/visibility)`. Above the horizon, `D = 0.006 + 0.994*max(0,sin((hour-6)*pi/12))`. Below it, `D = 0.006*exp(0.7*elevationDegrees)*smoothstep(-18,-12,elevationDegrees)`. This authored approximation retains a small ambient contribution at the horizon, decays through twilight and reaches exactly zero below -18 degrees. It decreases with depth/turbidity; it is not a measured twilight irradiance curve. Both height-fog additive colour and the underwater post-process use this source. The latter runs in scene-linear HDR before depth of field (and before bloom/tonemapping), with Unreal's pre-exposure conversion enabled. Nearby sediment is composed afterward so this opaque-background distance does not erase the cloud; see [SEDIMENT.md](SEDIMENT.md). Its illustrative asymptotic RGB source is `(250,1050,900)*A`; height fog uses `(280,1150,950)*A*cameraWaterBlend`. Extinction remains distinct from ambient source strength; the lit scene, including the torch, is attenuated rather than replaced by a fixed display-space green colour. These coefficients are calibrated rendering parameters, not a complete light-transport model. [Unreal post-process stages](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/post-process-materials-in-unreal-engine).

The manual camera explicitly uses ISO 100, f/4 and 1/60 s. Exposure compensation is `8.5-log2(10000*pow(A,0.6)+8)`, bounded at 5.5 in darkness; this gives partial compensation as ambient light fades, retaining a visible decline in brightness with depth and twilight without unlimited amplification of an unlit scene. Full inverse compensation overexposed the native twilight sky and was replaced after a rendered comparison. It is an immediate, deterministic gameplay adaptation, not a measured human-eye response or image-histogram metering. See [Unreal manual exposure](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/auto-exposure-in-unreal-engine). UE 5.8's `r.EyeAdaptation.CachedLightingPreExposure=8` covers approximately EV -4 to 16 in its Lumen/real-time-sky cache, encompassing this camera range. The default offset 4 produced a visible cache-clipping warning at daylight exposure and was therefore replaced.

## Waves and weather

`Simulation/WaterSurface.h` defines four linear deep-water components with wavelengths 20, 9, 4.5 and 2.1 m. Each uses `omega² = g k`, height `A sin(k·x - omega t + phase)`, analytical slopes and orbital water velocity. Dynamic pressure and orbital velocity decay as `exp(-k depth)`. Near the interface, pressure depth blends over 2 m to the local water column and is zero in air. This blend, the four-component spectrum, directional spread and amplitude `0.0025 × wind² × {1, .5, .25, .125}` are gameplay approximations. The linear dispersion and depth decay follow [MIT's linear water-wave notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-017j-design-of-electromechanical-robotic-systems-fall-2009/51e6a1ab5a7fa9937bdb7aca645897fd_MIT2_017JF09_ch06.pdf). No shallow-water dispersion, shoaling, breaking waves, fetch model or measured Walensee spectrum is claimed.

Wind ranges from 0 to 24 m/s; the direction is where it blows **toward**, clockwise from north. Above 12 m/s, all wavelengths grow by `(wind/12)^2`, matching amplitude growth and keeping component steepness at the 12 m/s bound. This authored extension is not a fetch-limited spectrum. The conservative sum-of-amplitudes bound is 0.675 m at 12 m/s, 2.26875 m at the 22 m/s storm preset and 2.7 m at the 24 m/s maximum; these are not significant wave heights. At zero wind, both geometry and micro-normal ripples are flat. The same component coefficients and elapsed simulation time drive CPU physics and the material. Rendering uses a 257 × 257 half-metre grid centred on a 32 m snapped viewer position, plus outer flat-water rings. Geometric displacement fades between 32 and 48 m from the viewer to avoid coarse distant tessellation. The central grid covers the entire displaced area even immediately before recentering. Its bounds include 2.9 m vertical shader displacement clearance. Procedural micro normals use three rotated, advected gradients of quintic-interpolated value noise instead of four globally repeating sine waves. Their cell scales are approximately 42, 19 and 8.5 cm; gradient weights are 0.025, 0.013 and 0.007, multiplied by bounded wind strength. Pixel derivatives attenuate unresolved detail; the simulation clock and primary wave direction drive advection. These are visual roughness details, not additional displaced or pressure-bearing waves. Native single-layer-water reflection denoising is enabled while the scene reflection method remains unchanged. Broad grazing-angle bands and the flat distant geometry remain visual limitations. Buoys and their anchor lines follow this same rendered heightfield.

Player drag includes wave orbital water velocity. Emerged body volume and the upper movement limit use the local moving interface. Gas pressure, displayed depth and ascent rate use pressure depth; short waves do not produce full-height pressure oscillations far below the surface. Breathing bubbles stop at the local interface and expand with local pressure. The wave clock freezes with a paused dive; the world updates after the pawn.

Clear, overcast, rain and storm are selectable scenario presets with default winds of 3, 5, 8 and 22 m/s. Selecting weather resets wind to its preset; subsequent manual wind edits survive sanitization, settings reload and profile round-trips. Weather identifiers 0–2 retain their meaning; storm is 3. The German storm preset lies within MeteoSwiss's Beaufort 9 range of 20.8–24.4 m/s ([table](https://www.meteoschweiz.admin.ch/dam/jcr%3Af348b5a7-dbac-431f-8539-689dc898afcb/Beaufort_Tabelle_de.pdf)); it is an authored scenario, not measured Walensee weather. A game-local copy of Unreal's default volumetric-cloud material changes cloud coverage/density, alongside sun/skylight intensity and atmospheric fog. Rain uses at most 1,600 instanced streaks (2,400 in storm) in a 12 m volume near the viewer, advected with wind and removed beneath the water surface. Their unlit emission compensates exposure for readability; this is not physically validated precipitation/lighting. Weather, wind, temperature profile, visibility, current and hour persist in settings and each dive's JSON environment metadata. Weather, wind and temperature inputs remain fixed during a dive; hour is the selected start time. The storm scenario increases rain audio gain to 1.4 while wind gain stays bounded at 1; existing underwater attenuation applies. Storm cloud coverage/density are 0.90/0.018, with direct/diffuse light multipliers 0.12/0.25. Lightning and thunder, live weather, surface splash/foam effects and reference-based optical acceptance remain open. The local scenario hour advances one second per simulated second from the configured start hour, wraps at midnight and freezes when elapsed simulation time freezes (pause or completed dive). Sun orientation/intensity, atmospheric lighting, underwater ambient attenuation and camera exposure follow this same clock. The HUD shows its current hour/minute. Start hour and elapsed duration already persist in the dive log, so no storage schema change is needed. The 06:00/18:00 sunrise/sunset approximation remains unchanged: calendar date, latitude/longitude, seasonal day length, moon and stars are not yet modeled.

These are gameplay parameters, not population estimates or locally measured habitat maps. Fish occurrence is supported by the [Eawag Projet Lac Walensee report, 2014](https://www.gl.ch/public/upload/assets/3028/Projet_Lacs_-_Walensee_Schlussbericht_def.pdf?fp=1). The whitefish model represents the **Coregonus group**, not a taxonomically validated model of a particular Walensee species.

Terrain look-ahead, missing-data rejection and conservative rock exclusion volumes constrain movement. Feeding, reproduction and seasonal ecology remain open. ### Fish geometry and surface detail

The five models now use smooth body profiles, attached caudal fans, subdivided fins, gill margins, mouth seams and separate eyes. Perch has two dorsal fins, a spiny leading fin, a dark posterior dorsal patch and flank bars; roach has a red iris; pike has an elongated head and posterior dorsal/anal fins; the whitefish group has an adipose fin; burbot has a rounded tail, long rear dorsal/anal fins and a chin barbel. Model lengths remain 30/24/75/38/55 cm before individual scaling.

The morphology review used the identification descriptions and photographs on printed pages 86 and 93 of [Inland Fisheries Ireland's 2019 ageing manual](https://www.fisheriesireland.ie/sites/default/files/migrated/docman/ifish/IFISH_VOL1_Manual%20on%20Ageing_2019-v2.pdf), the two whitefish forms illustrated in figure 4-12 on printed page 15 of the [Eawag Walensee report](https://www.fischereiberatung.ch/fileadmin/sites/fiber/angebot/andere_publ/projet_lac/projet_lac_walensee.pdf), and the [British Columbia burbot conservation strategy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/plants-animals-and-ecosystems/fish-fish-habitat/fishery-resources/region-4-kootenay-boundary/finalburbotcs-3-2-05.pdf). These references informed authored shapes; no source photograph pixels were copied into game assets, and the meshes are not specimen scans or measured reconstructions. The whitefish taxonomy remains explicitly a group.

Each model has original 2048×2048 color, DirectX normal and surface-property atlases, with staggered scale margins, lateral-line detail, species-dependent bars/spots/mottling, fin rays and eye regions. Burbot's embedded scales are deliberately much less visible. The shared lit material uses roughness variation and a subsurface approximation for the fins. These are designed optical parameters, not measured fish-skin properties. [FISH_TEXTURES.json](FISH_TEXTURES.json) records the source maps and hashes; [FISH_MESH_AUDIT.json](FISH_MESH_AUDIT.json) verifies finite geometry, length, polygon areas, atlas coordinates and animation weights.

Four generated mesh LODs target 100%, 45%, 18% and 6.5% of triangles, with screen-size thresholds 1.0/0.35/0.12/0.04. Texture mipmaps stream normally. Tail deformation uses vertex alpha weights and per-instance world-side vectors, species-dependent cycles and escape rate. Accumulated animation phase avoids jumps when speed changes and pauses with the simulation. The FBX reimport policy is explicitly set on both task and existing asset: retaining an old IGNORE policy discarded tint and movement weights, which is now rejected by asset/runtime checks. This remains procedural vertex animation; independent fin articulation, gill ventilation, mouth opening, physiological skin optics, expert anatomical review and photorealistic acceptance remain open.

## First-person equipment and wrist display

`ADiveEquipment` uses the articulated diver's hand transforms for the wrist-computer and torch meshes. C raises/lowers the left forearm with an interpolated pose; R cycles four pages while raised. The display remains available when the screen HUD is hidden with H. The selected wet/dry suit changes sleeve width and colour. The torch's spotlight originates at its visible lens and uses the lamp switch/battery state. Body pose and equipment collision are described in BODY_POSE.md and CONTACTS.md; anatomical refinement remains open.

The computer is a world-space surface in the housing recess, driven by a 768 × 512 canvas render target. It snapshots the existing simulation state at approximately 10 Hz; it has no separate physiology or gas model. All pages retain depth, tank pressure and elapsed time; the first three also show heading. The dive page adds NDL, ascent rate, maximum depth, water temperature and stop timer; the vital page adds pulse, SpO2, respiration, core temperature, stress and fatigue; the gas page adds ppO2, ambient pressure, estimated gas time to 50 bar, ceiling and lamp charge; the fourth page adds the ascent forecast. Exploration is explicitly shown as unlimited gas. The footer displays the highest-priority active warning. English/German labels follow the game language.

Exposure-compensated emission keeps the display readable in dark water. Numeric pixels are rendered after temporal reconstruction to avoid trails from changing digits. Because Unreal's [after-motion-blur translucency pass](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/MaterialTranslucencyPass?application_version=5.4) disables hardware depth testing, the material explicitly compares scene and surface depth with a 1 mm tolerance. Automated wrist scenarios cycle all four pages with the HUD hidden, save main/vital/forecast screenshots, export the source display texture and check that the render callback ran, all pages were drawn, the wrist reached its raised pose and the state snapshot is no more than 0.5 s old. These checks do not replace visual review of readability, distortion or rendering artifacts.

## Zustandsgekoppeltes Audio

`Simulation/AudioModel.h` erzeugt Einatmungsereignisse für den eingesetzten, versorgten Regler beim Phasenwechsel und Ausatmung bei Atemphase 0,52. Die Atemfrequenz stammt unmittelbar aus `DiveModel`; Tonhöhe und Dauer der Reglerklänge folgen dieser Frequenz. Lautstärke reagiert auf Anstrengung. Bei leerer Flasche beginnt kein neuer Atemzug; ein bereits eingeatmeter letzter Atemzug kann noch ausgeatmet werden. Sichtbare Blasen und der räumliche Blasenton nutzen dasselbe Ereignis. Über Wasser entstehen keine sichtbaren Atemblasen. Absetzen des Reglers unterbricht dessen Atemereignisse; Umgebungsluft hat derzeit noch keinen eigenen Atemklang. Der Mundpunkt entscheidet über Blasenerzeugung, die Kameratiefe über akustische Dämpfung.

Der doppelte Herzton wird einmal je simuliertem Herzzyklus gestartet. Er wird bei Stress/Belastung deutlicher und besitzt einen eigenen Lautstärkeregler. Dies ist ein Komfort-/Feedbackeffekt, keine validierte Körperakustik. Das Jacket liefert tatsächlichen Ein-/Auslassfluss in lokalen Litern pro Sekunde einschließlich Überdruckablass. Leere Gasquelle bzw. leeres Jacket erzeugen keinen fiktiven Ventilfluss.

`UDiveAudio` verwaltet elf Kanäle. Ein Blasencluster startet 25 cm vor/15 cm unter dem Kopf und steigt mit 0,3 m/s im Weltkoordinatensystem auf; Distanzabschwächung und Stereoortung erfolgen durch Unreal. Die übrigen körpernahen Signale bleiben im Kopfhörermittelpunkt. Wasser-, Oberflächen-, Wind- und Regenkulisse sind unabhängige Stereoschleifen. Die Überblendung folgt der Kameratiefe unter der tatsächlichen Wellenoberfläche über 0–0,5 m. Oberflächengeräusche nehmen darunter exponentiell mit 3 m Skala ab; der Tiefpass läuft von 20 kHz auf 650 Hz. Dies sind abgestimmte Spielparameter, kein Schallausbreitungsmodell im See.

Neue Warnungen erzeugen einen Doppelton mit zwei Sekunden Schutz vor schnellem Wiederholen. Ein neuer Leerflaschenalarm umgeht diese Sperre. Fehlende Atemluft alarmiert beim ersten Auftreten sofort; weitere neue Atemluftverluste haben eine eigene achtsekündige Tonsperre gegen Wellenflattern. Die sichtbare Warnung bleibt unabhängig davon aktiv. Anhaltende Warnungen bleiben visuell sichtbar, ohne in jedem Simulationsschritt zu piepen. Gesamt-, Umgebungs-, Ausrüstungs-, Herz- und Warnlautstärke werden multipliziert, auf 0–1 begrenzt und in den Einstellungen gespeichert. Pause hält sämtliche Quellen und Ereignisphasen an; Fortsetzen führt den Klang fort.

Die 48-kHz-PCM-Quellen entstehen deterministisch in `Tools/create_audio.py`. Schleifen verwenden eine 250-ms-Überblendung statt einer regelmäßigen stillen Stelle. Fremdaufnahmen werden nicht verwendet. Realistische Klangreferenzen, klangliche Abnahme auf Kopfhörern/Lautsprechern sowie eine detaillierte Unterwasserakustik bleiben offen.

Der Audiotest zeichnet den tatsächlich gerenderten Mastermix über [Unreals Submix-Aufnahme](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/AudioMixer/UAudioMixerBlueprintLibrary/StopRecordingOutput) auf. Er misst Signal, Übersteuerung, Pause, Stummschaltung und Wiederaufnahme. Diese technische Prüfung ersetzt keine Hörabnahme.

## Steuerung und Einführung

`FDiveControls` ist die gemeinsame Quelle für die 17 Aktionsbelegungen und ihre Anzeigen. `ADivePlayerController` verarbeitet Einzeldrücke, Tastenauswahl, das feste Escape und F1 über Unreals `FInputKeyEventArgs`. Bewegung/Jacket lesen denselben Schlüsselbestand als gehaltene Tasten; Mausachsen bleiben im Engine-Inputsystem. Eine Konfliktbelegung tauscht Aktionen, statt eine davon unbedienbar zu machen. Ungültige, doppelte und reservierte Einträge aus gespeicherten Einstellungen werden abgefangen. Die Einzel-Tastenbelegung unterstützt Tastatur und digitale Maustasten; Chords, Analogachsen und Gamepads sind hier noch nicht implementiert.

Die Steuerungsseite ist auch während einer Pause verfügbar. Pause und Änderungen geben gehaltene Eingaben über `FlushPressedKeys` frei. Sensitivität ist auf 0,03–0,5 begrenzt, das Sichtfeld auf 60–110 Grad. Alte Einstellungen ohne neue Felder verwenden die bisherigen Standards. Speicherung erfolgt über eine temporäre Datei und anschließendes Ersetzen; fehlgeschlagenes Speichern wird angezeigt.

`Simulation/Tutorial.h` prüft konkrete Aktionen: mindestens 25 Grad aufsummierte Blickbewegung, drei Meter Bewegung mit Schwimmeingabe (Strömungsdrift zählt nicht), mindestens 1,5 lokale Liter Jacket-Zufuhr und anschließende Abgabe, geöffneter Computer auf der Vitalwerteseite, Lampe aus/ein, Karte auf/zu sowie Rückkehr auf höchstens 0,8 m Tiefe innerhalb zwölf Metern zur Boje. Erst das erfolgreiche Beenden schließt die Einführung ab. Die Darstellung verwendet jederzeit die aktuelle Tastenbelegung. Der Übungstauchgang hat einen eigenen ruhigen Weesen-Tauchplan; Speichern während der Übung bewahrt den vorherigen normalen Plan. Abschluss und Abbruch stellen diesen Plan wieder her.

Die Gameplay-Tests erzeugen simulierte Unreal-Eingabeereignisse, keine Desktop-Tastendrücke. Reale Eingaben werden vom Testcontroller ignoriert. Der Belegungstest prüft den Weg über Controller, HUD-Tastenauswahl, gespeicherte Einstellungen und tatsächliche Schwimmbewegung. Der Einführungstest durchläuft alle acht Aufgaben im laufenden Spiel und liest Abschluss, Tauchlog sowie ursprünglichen Tauchplan wieder ein. Diese automatisierten Prüfungen ersetzen keine manuelle Prüfung mit verschiedenen Tastaturlayouts.

## Gespeicherte Tauchkonfigurationen

`DiveConfigIO` serialisiert sämtliche Felder von `Dive::Config` sowie den Einstieg. Einstellungen aus früheren Versionen dürfen Felder auslassen und erhalten sichere Standards; Profile verlangen dagegen vollständige, endliche und gültige Werte. Additive spätere Felder dürfen in älteren Profilen fehlen: `start_on_shore` erhält dann false und `air_temperature_c` 20 °C. Vorhandene ungültige Werte werden abgewiesen. Ungültige Profile werden nicht stillschweigend in eine andere Konfiguration umgewandelt. Sauerstoffanteil, Oberflächendruck und automatische Tarierung werden mitgespeichert, auch wenn das Menü dafür noch keinen eigenen Regler bietet. Die Profilvorschau zeigt diese Werte.

`FDiveProfiles` speichert eine Sammlung mit Schema 1 in `Saved/DiveProfiles.json`: stabile GUID, Name, UTC-Zeit, Konfiguration, Archivkennzeichen und optional der vorherige Name/Konfigurationsstand. Maximal 32 Profile dürfen aktiv sein, maximal 128 insgesamt. Namen umfassen 1–40 UTF-16-Codeeinheiten, unterstützen Unicode und sind ohne Beachtung der Groß-/Kleinschreibung eindeutig. Namen werden nie als Dateipfade benutzt. Archivieren verändert keine Simulation und löscht keine Datensätze. Der vorherige Stand bleibt auf der Festplatte erhalten; erneutes Wiederherstellen tauscht die beiden Stände.

Der Parser begrenzt Dateien auf 512 KiB, prüft Metadaten, Dubletten und alle Konfigurationsfelder und übernimmt nur vollständig gültige Sammlungen. Schreibfehler setzen den Zustand im Speicher zurück. Eine nicht lesbare Datei wird nicht überschrieben. Vor dem Schreiben wird der Dateiinhalt mit dem zuletzt gelesenen Stand verglichen; bereits gespeicherte Änderungen anderer Instanzen lösen einen Konflikthinweis aus. Dies ist eine lokale Konflikterkennung, keine Datenbanktransaktion für zeitgleich schreibende Prozesse. Die Datei wird über eine temporäre Datei ersetzt.

Die Profilverwaltung verwendet Canvas für Liste und Vorschau sowie einen fokussierten Slate-Textdialog für Namen. Ein offener Dialog erhält Texteingaben, Enter bestätigt und Escape bricht ab. Laden ist nur vor bzw. nach einem Tauchgang möglich; während eines aktiven Tauchgangs bleiben Simulation und Plan geschützt. Audio, Sprache und Tastenbelegung sind allgemeine Einstellungen und werden durch Profile nicht verändert. Der automatisierte Menülauf verwendet ausschließlich `DiveProfilesTest.json` und `DiveSettingsTest.json`.
